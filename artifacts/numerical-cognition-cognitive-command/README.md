# From Numerical Cognition to Cognitive Command

*A research note on two requirements for basic arithmetic*

## Question and intended use

What further argument is needed to connect the cognitive support of elementary arithmetic to a particular criterion of mathematical objectivity? Philosophy-interested readers can inspect the distinction between learning arithmetic and justifying its standards, the reconstruction of Pantsar’s proposal, and the strongest objection and conditional reply. This is a separate inquiry from the Ledger’s experiential-comparison paper.

## Claim supported

The note distinguishes two requirements within the cognitive-command approach discussed by Shapiro: a warranted exclusion of relevant blameless disagreement and a justified way of locating the error. Evidence about acquisition, widespread agreement, or a single identifiable mistake does not by itself establish both. Elementary checking provides a plausible reply conditional on shared numerical meanings and warranted checking standards. This is an analytical reconstruction, not an established refutation of Pantsar or a claim of theoretical novelty.

## Results and unresolved issues

The result is a bounded clarification of an inferential burden. It does not settle whether practices help establish standards, help acquire or justify them, or contribute in several ways. Concept formation remains open. The author’s uncertainty on this point, discussed during development as “C”, is not assent to any single answer.

The strongest objection asks how cognitive constraints acquire normative authority and warrant correction. The strongest reply permits developmental explanation and conceptual justification to work together, but assumes shared content and warranted checking. How practices establish or justify those standards remains unresolved.

The note does not show that locating error is necessary for mathematical objectivity generally, that an alternative ontology is false, that language alone causes exact concepts, or that Pantsar’s account fails. The examples concern familiar finite calculations; they do not cover every arithmetic proposition, proof dispute or culturally different concept. No new empirical case, data analysis or systematic review is included.

**Unresolved question:** How, if at all, do learned practices help establish or justify elementary arithmetic’s checking standards, beyond helping people acquire them?
