# Source map: BFT first-event prime census

## Primary source

Banks, William D.; Ford, Kevin; Tao, Terence. “Large prime gaps and probabilistic models.” *Inventiones mathematicae* (2023). DOI: [10.1007/s00222-023-01199-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-023-01199-0). The open publisher record and article describe a random-residue-class sieve model for studying large prime gaps.

The present finite experiment uses a BFT first-event calculation as one candidate proposal. The cited paper is mathematical background for the model family; it does not assert the deterministic candidate-ranking interpretation tested here. The relation between the source model and this operationalisation is therefore an inference and should not be read as a claim made by the authors.

## Exact bounded claim

For the fixed frame 10,050,248–10,100,247, over 3,111 eligible prime origins, the frozen BFT proposal equalled the first modulus-210 survivor at every origin and had 807 exact-next hits. Two frozen gap-median comparators had 409 and 487 exact-next hits. The result is descriptive because a status read exposed some origin labels during execution.

## Prior-work comparison

The cited source studies probabilistic models for large gaps. This experiment asks a different bounded application question: whether one first-event proposal ranks the immediate next prime better than specified gap-median rules, and whether it differs from the first modulus-210 survivor. A bounded search of scholarly results for BFT next-prime ranking and related candidate-ranking terms found no direct equivalent, but this was not a systematic review. No priority or novelty claim is made.
